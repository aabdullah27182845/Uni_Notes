
In these notes, we will prove that Regular Languages are Context Free.

For us to do that, we have to define a new term, called ***right-linear***. A CFG is is ***right-linear*** if every rule is either of the form $R \to wT$ or of the form $R \to w$ where $w$ ranges over the strings of terminals, and $R$ and $T$ over variables.

The following is a **Theorem**: *A language is regular if and only if it is generated by a right-linear CFG*.

The idea of this proof is as follows: We need to define yet another term. Say a CFG is ***strongly right-linear*** if each rule has the following forms: 
- $R \to aT$
- $R \to T$
- $R \to \epsilon$
where $a$ ranges over terminals, and $R$ and $T$ over variables.

Now, we can show that every right-linear CFG is also a strongly right-linear CFG, with some decomposition and substitutions. For each rule $R \to wT$, with $w = a_0a_1...a_n$, we add $n$ fresh variables, say $V_1,..., V_n$ and replace the rule $R \to wT$ with $$R\to a_0V_1, \quad V_1\to a_1V_2, \quad, ..., \quad V_n \to a_nT$$kind of like we are "unravelling" our string of terminals and linking them up with a chain of rules in order to define the exact same thing.

With this, we can start to define equivalences with some given CFGs and NFAs. 

"$\xRightarrow{}$" ($A \to B$): We map DFAs $M = (Q, \Sigma, \delta, q_0, F)$ to strongly right-linear CFGs $G_M = (Q, \Sigma, \mathcal{R}, q_0)$ where $$
\begin{align*}
q \rightarrow a \ q' \in \mathcal{R} &\iff \delta(q, a) = q' \\
q \rightarrow \epsilon \in \mathcal{R} &\iff q \in F
\end{align*}
$$
"$\xLeftarrow{}$" ($B \to A$): We map strongly right-linear CFGs $G = (V, \Sigma, \mathcal{R}, S)$ to NFAs $N_G = (V, \Sigma, \delta, S, F)$ where $$
\begin{align*}
R \xrightarrow{a} R' \ (\text{i.e. } R' \in \delta(R, a)) &\iff R \rightarrow a \ R' \in \mathcal{R} \\
R \xrightarrow{\epsilon} R' \ (\text{i.e. } R' \in \delta(R, \epsilon)) &\iff R \rightarrow R' \in \mathcal{R} \\
R \in F &\iff R \rightarrow \epsilon \in \mathcal{R}
\end{align*}
$$
And with that, our proof is completed.
